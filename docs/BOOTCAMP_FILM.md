# "55 Spring Festivals" (五十五个春节): the bootcamp film

Adapted from team Proxima's stage-1 comic, which is the team's own story: Mikhil Naika, Yorvan Rengasamy, Seeya Awotar. **The comic's artwork was made with Muse Image before the bootcamp, so none of it may be used, even as a reference image.** Every image must be generated from scratch during 10:40–12:00. The story, names and dialogue are the team's own and are fine.

## The story (from the comic)

- **1972:** Wei Lam, 14, leaves Meizhou (Hakka, Guangdong) with a string-tied cardboard suitcase. He comes to Uncle Lam's basalt-stone shop "LAM STORE 林記" among the sugar cane, with Pieter Both mountain behind. He is homesick and writes letters he can't afford to send. Smriti, a girl from across the road, throws him a cricket ball: "Nobody knows the rules. Just run!"
- **Every Spring Festival, Smriti's father takes a photo at the shop door.** Photo No. 1: a stiff uncle and a nervous boy.
- **1979:** Uncle Lam hands Wei the keys: "Take care of this street, and it will take care of you."
- **1984:** Wei marries Smriti; the whole street comes. Dumplings and dholl puri; the lion dance in Chinatown. In 1990 the shop is full of running children. Photo No. 18 is crowded with neighbours nobody invited.
- **2008:** The children have moved to town, Uncle Lam is gone, and it's raining. A phone call: Wei's mother is very ill. In Meizhou, after 36 years, he is the stranger. "I don't know where I belong anymore, Ma." / "Home is where your children laugh. Go back to them." He comes home to a "WELCOME HOME" banner: "We kept the lights on."
- **2027, the 55th Spring Festival:** the shop is restored with its basalt walls showing, and one long table for every community. A video call: "Happy Spring Festival, Meizhou!" / "Happy Spring Festival, Mauritius!" Photo No. 55 needs a wider lens. His granddaughter Mira wins a scholarship to China. At the airport she carries the same suitcase. "It brought me here. Let it bring you back." End card: **55 years. Two homes. One family.**

## Film devices (what lifts it above the comic)

1. **The photo flash as the time-cut.** Each "Spring Festival photo No. X" ends on a camera flash going white, and the next era comes out of the flash. That gives the film a rhythm and a strong recurring motif.
2. **The time-lapse "wow" shot: the shop through 55 years in one continuous move.** This is FLUX 3 Max keyframes-to-video with four Muse Image keyframes from the same camera position: shop 1972, 1984, 2008, 2027. Each keyframe is painted by editing the previous one, which was the comic's own technique. The frangipani tree grows, the second floor goes up, the sky turns over. It's the centrepiece, and the innovation pitch's best example.
3. **Wei ages by edits.** As in the comic, each older Wei is Muse Image editing the previous Wei: 14, 21, 26, 32, 50, 69. The same goes for Smriti. Character sheets are made first in the session.
4. **Kinetic moments for the cinematic, action style the user wants,** without breaking the story's tone:
   - the cricket game: a fast tracking shot, the ball flying past the camera;
   - the lion dance in Chinatown: handheld in the crowd, firecrackers, drums;
   - the 2008 storm: rain lashing, a whip pan to the ringing phone;
   - the dash to the airport, Mira running, the plane taking off;
   - an FPV drone flight over the 2027 long table.
5. **Sound:** FLUX makes the effects in each clip (cane rustling, the cricket bat crack, firecrackers, lion-dance drums, rain, the airport). One score runs over the whole film (ElevenLabs Music): a Hakka-flavoured erhu or dizi melody meeting sega ravanne rhythm, which is itself the "two homes" idea in sound. Short dialogue lines go in the FLUX prompts as a named speaker; the narration ("I was fourteen…") is optional voiceover.

## Content checks (rules)

- Nothing political or religious: no temples or deities as subjects, and no flags used as statements. The lion dance is cultural, which is fine.
- The text in the frame is the shop sign "LAM STORE 林記" and the WELCOME HOME banner. Muse Image may garble them, so it may be better to add the titles and captions in the edit (allowed: non-AI text).

## Language and subtitles

- **English audio:** every dialogue line and any voiceover is in English, generated in the FLUX clip (a named visible speaker) or as AI voiceover during the session.
- **Chinese subtitles:** a simplified-Chinese line under every English line, plus the end card "55 years. Two homes. One family." in both languages. They are burned in with ffmpeg during the edit, from an `.srt` timed to the cut. Who translates (a teammate or an AI) goes in the AI Creation Statement.
- Keep the clip frames clear of in-frame text so the subtitles stay readable.

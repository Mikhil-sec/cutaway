# The Dodo Came Back

Category B (AI) entry for the MFDC National Short Film Competition 2026, theme "What If the Dodo Returns?".

## Brief

- Length: about 150 seconds (must be between 90 and 180).
- Wordless: no dialogue and no voice narration. The story is told through images, music, ambient sound, a few sparse text cards and one end card (see "Text cards" below).
- Style: muted cinematic anime, painterly 2D backgrounds, soft natural light.
- One dodo is the protagonist for most of the film. Many dodos appear only in the reveal near the end.
- Real Mauritian places, recognisable but repainted: Port Louis streets, the Caudan Waterfront, the harbour, Chinatown, Champ de Mars with Le Pouce and the Moka range behind.
- No real brands, logos or legible real shop names. Dodo images on souvenirs and signs are generic.
- Humans appear as passers-by and tourists; no close-ups of faces.

## Text cards (approved by the author, 2026-11-01)

The cards stand in for narration. Keep them sparse: show, don't tell.
- **Time and place cards:**
  - "Mauritius, 1681." over the forest opening. That was about the last recorded sighting of a dodo, so the audience knows at once what they are watching.
  - "Port Louis. Today." over the waking in the city. It explains the jump across the centuries.
- **At most one or two quiet lines at key turns**, for example "It was not the only one." at the reveal of the many dodos behind glass.
- **The end card** stays: "The dodo came back. Its home didn't."
- **Look:** EB Garamond, the same font as the end card. Soft fade in and out, low in the frame, never over the action. English only, no Kreol.

## Story

1. **The forest (0–15 s).** Evening in the ancient Mauritian forest, mist between ebony trees, warm pink-gold light. The dodo stands on a mossy rock, at home. As the light fades to blue dusk, it settles down to sleep on the same rock, and the forest fades around it. It sleeps through the night, and through the centuries. (Changed from dawn to evening on 2026-11-01, so that falling asleep makes sense and the light can carry the time passing.)
2. **Waking (15–30 s).** The dodo wakes on the same mossy rock, which still sits in a small roadside garden bed on a Port Louis pavement: the city grew around it while it slept. Traffic, buses, people hurrying past. It startles, and flees down a narrow Chinatown back street, glancing behind it. (Changed on 2026-11-02: it used to wake on bare pavement and then stroll calmly down the same street, which made no sense after the fright.)
3. **The search (30–60 s).** It wanders the city looking for its forest: the Chinatown gate, the waterfront canal with Le Pouce rising behind the buildings. Its own image is everywhere, on souvenirs, a mural, a shop sign, but there is not a single tree it knows. From a height it sees the mountains far away, beyond the city.
4. **The attraction (60–80 s).** People notice it. Phones come out; a crowd gathers under the colourful hanging umbrellas at the Caudan Waterfront. The dodo is surrounded, and then it is shut in a wooden crate in front of them. (The capture shot was added on 2026-11-02.)
5. **Taken (80–105 s).** It is carried away in a crate past the harbour cranes, and brought to an attraction called Dodo Park. The reveal: many dodos behind a glass wall, visitors holding up phones, camera flashes.
6. **The forest that's left (105–135 s).** On the enclosure's back wall is a faded painted mural of the forest. The dodo walks up to it and presses against it, the only forest it will see again.
7. **End card (135–150 s).** Fade to black. One line of text: "The dodo came back. Its home didn't."
